"THE  author  of  the  Shepherd  of  Hermas 
nowhere  supplies  us  with  a  direct  quotation 
from  the  Old  or  New  Testament,  and  we  are 
therefore  obliged  to  fall  back  upon  allusions 
which  always  admit  of  some  degree  of  doubt. 
He  may  sometimes  be  consciously  borrowing 
ideas  from  New  Testament  writers  when  the 
reference  is  veiled  by  an  intentional  change  of 
words;  and  sometimes  he  may  use  identical 
words,  and  yet  have  derived  them  from  some 
other  source,  oral  or  written.  In  these  circum- 
stances it  is  clear  that  references  which  might 
reasonably  be  assumed  if  we  knew  that  the 
author  was  familiar  with  our  canonical  books, 
cannot  be  used  to  establish  his  familiarity  with
